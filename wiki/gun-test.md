# Gun Test

Self-check before play: **Would you trust yourself with a gun right now?** If not, should you be playing at MEDIUM [[severity-model|severity]] where unconscious mistakes have real consequences?

---

## The Logic

A gun operates at HIGH severity — one mistake is permanent. Intimacy operates at MEDIUM — one mistake has real impact. If you wouldn't trust yourself with a weapon, question whether you should be wielding intimacy.

Reasons you might fail the Gun Test:
- Sleeping meds or substances affecting awareness
- Emotional flooding / distress
- Exhaustion
- Hunger (physical or emotional)
- Activated trauma

Failing doesn't mean you can't participate. It means **choose consciously** — adjust what you do, tell your partner, take precautions. Don't sleepwalk.

---

## The State Check (on Others)

The Gun Test is the question you ask yourself; the **state check** is how you ask someone else to run it. Walk up and say **"state check"** — the word "gun" is never spoken. Spoken words land on everyone in earshot, and "gun" at a play party installs threat in participants who don't know the tool ([[trauma-and-filters]]). There's no context-switching (staff-speak vs. participant-speak): autopilot says the words you practice regardless of room ([[mistake-matrix]] — Target Focus Training's weapon-drop rule). The gun stays on the page, where it teaches; "state check" is what goes in the air. Naming lineage (memory bridge): the question asks about your *state* and a *gun* → full name "gun state check" → you speak the last half. **"Gun Check" as a tool name is retired (2026-07-05)** — the interpersonal check is only ever called the state check.

**Answering a check follows the same language rule:** run the question silently, reply gun-free — *"I'm good — thanks for checking"* or *"Good catch. I wouldn't pass right now — how do you think we should proceed?"*

The check itself (steps 3–4 only if the container allows continuing and the person wants to):
1. Acknowledge what you noticed
2. Check whether continuing is even on the table (container rules; the person's own preference)
3. Inform their partner — participant-safe language, script self-translates the term ("wouldn't pass a state check — which means they're foggier than they'd like to be")
4. Probe the partner's response plan: "If they make a mistake while foggy, what will you do?" A freeze, a blank, or *"honestly, I'd be furious"* is data — can justify pausing regardless of container rules on the fog

**If they need to stop** (container prohibits, or someone judges it right): delivered as a pause, never punishment — the person told the truth on a state check, and shaming truth teaches the next person to lie. Script promises no comeback window (break length is the container's call: cool-down vs. rest of event). The delivery's attention matters more than its words — attention on "problem to manage" lands as verdict, attention on "looking out for both people" lands as care. For staff being stopped: the no protects *them* most ([[top-and-bottom-vulnerability]] — their mistakes land at higher severity). The whole exchange runs on the trust question from healing-fawning ("what action creates the most trust right now?"): admission received as care → more admissions; received as a strike → silence, and the container finds out about problems only after they happen.

Don't be a pest. But don't ignore what you see.
