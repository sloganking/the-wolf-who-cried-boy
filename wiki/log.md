## [2026-07-03] add | `notes/writing-guide.md` — new core value "Stand on Ground You Actually Hold" + intro flow fix

**Writing-guide change (author-directed):** Codified a value that previously lived only in conversation: no posturing, no borrowed authority — any sentence designed to make the author look like something he isn't carries a hidden "they can't find out" rule, and hidden rules manufacture fear (pattern extracted from the author's private notes: concealment rules make every interaction a place the secret could escape; transparency deletes the fear's engine, not manages it — the author lived both sides of this while building the coaching practice). The value: declare the vantage, then speak with full authority from it. Worked example cited: "Where I Stand" states the exact fact a posturing author would hide and reads stronger for it. Test: would the passage weaken if the best-informed, least-friendly reader questioned it? Two guard rails: declaring ground once ≠ hedging repeatedly (that's fear wearing honesty's clothes), and the book's authority is demonstrated sight, not credentials. Also added to the finalization checklist.

**Book change (same session):** In the intro's "A Note on Tone," restored "If that lands wrong at first, sit with it" to direct adjacency with the fix-blindness-with-sight quote it responds to (item B's inserted register paragraph had separated them); the register paragraph now closes the section, ending on "Weigh it against your own."

**Files updated:** `notes/writing-guide.md` (new value #7 + checklist line), `src/introduction.md` (paragraph order), `wiki/log.md` (this entry).

**Extended same session (2nd):** Value #2 (Show, Don't Tell) was a three-sentence stub; enriched with the author's July 2026 distinctions from the private repo, pattern-level: **"Telling takes trust. Showing creates it"** (a told claim isn't self-evident, so the reader must advance trust the author may not have earned; showing keeps the cost on the author — expensive to fake, therefore credible; told conclusions spend trust, shown patterns deposit it); **told knowledge is hearsay** (the body's court only admits what was witnessed — story is the closest thing to lived experience a page can deliver, which is why major principles should arrive as scenes before sentences); **the blacklight test** (truths stated up front get rejected; shown journeys make the same sentences land as "language for things I've always known" — write so the reader arrives, name it after they've seen it); plus three practical checks (verifiable-from-the-page vs. requires-trust; has the reader watched it happen before it's stated; if a passage is arguing, it's telling).

**Extended same session:** (1) Value #7 gained a "moves" block with worked examples: the credibility inversion (include the sentence a scared author would cut), the motive check (a clause that exists to impress is the tell — with the real thousand-dollars-an-hour deliberation recorded), no fictional present (never speak from a position not currently held), and rates-vs-claims (a price you set is inarguable ground; a track record is a checkable claim). (2) First enforcement of the value in the book: `before-you-facilitate.md`'s two false-present staff passages ("When I require my staff... my events" in the Jumpmaster Reframe; "If my staff are consuming predator-hunting content..." in the horror rule) rewritten to the reader's voice — the author does not currently have staff or events, and the fictional present was exactly the exposure the value exists to prevent.
