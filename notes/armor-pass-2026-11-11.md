## How this was built

**Shapes searched (the brief's list):** this isn't, this is not, that isn't, that is not, this doesn't mean, that doesn't mean, not because, none of this, to be clear, I'm not saying, I am not saying, this is not to say, doesn't make, isn't about, is not about, not an excuse, not a license. "To be clear", "I'm not saying", "I am not saying", "this is not to say" and "not an excuse" do not occur in `src/`.

**Shapes added:** that's not / it's not / it isn't; doesn't mean / does not mean; this doesn't / that doesn't; aren't excuses; not a loophole; not permission; not a character flaw; not a moral failing; I'm not (telling, asking, citing); I wasn't (trying, being); you're not (blaming, saying, evil, weak, crazy, uniquely); sentence fragments that open with "Not" ("Not consciously.", "Not as punishment"); the point / goal / question / problem isn't; isn't prohibition; doesn't automatically mean; this chapter isn't; and twelve denial-first sentences noticed in the read that no pattern caught.

**What was left out, and why.** The patterns matched 784 sentences. 330 of them are judged below, with the twelve from the read making 342. The other 454 were read and set aside under one rule: the sentence is not defending anything. They are (a) plain negation inside a description ("something that isn't actually a threat"), (b) quoted speech, scripts' inner monologue and film dialogue, or (c) the book's teaching contrast, where the thing denied is the reader's actual first reading and the denial is the lesson ("That's not repair. That's submission."; "The Inquisitors weren't cartoon villains."; "Not from the angry partner."). Class (c) is the largest. It is the book's signature construction: "that's not" appears 110 times and sentence fragments opening with "Not" about 278 times. Each one is doing work. Together they set a rhythm of correction, and that rhythm is a second, slower armor question for a later pass: how many reclassifications per page before the reader feels corrected instead of shown.

**How each was judged.** From its paragraph and from the whole book, not from the sentence. KEEP: without it a careful reader really would draw a wrong and harmful conclusion (safety, legal, consent, or the blame boundary where the claim is strongest), or the denial is itself the lesson. CUT: the surrounding text already makes the point. TURN: the same content as a statement of what is true, shorter than the original. A boundary the book has already drawn three times is a CUT or TURN even where it would be a KEEP on first appearance. The largest repeated families: the blame/fault boundary (about fifteen statements), "not stupid, not malicious, not evil" (about twenty), "not punishment" (about ten), "not perfection" (four), the Gun Test's not-a-ban point (four on one page).

**Voice rule applied.** Still precise and inarguable; the sword stays. Nothing below softens a claim. Each CUT or TURN removes a defense against a charge the reader has not made, and leaves the claim standing alone.

**Reading an entry.** Line number · heading · verdict · the sentence exactly as it stands (emphasis markers dropped where a fragment would leave them unbalanced) · one line of reason · for TURN, the rewrite after the arrow. Where a rewrite replaces more than one sentence, the whole span is quoted.
