# New Chapter Brief: When It's Actually a Wolf

**Status:** Not yet written. Brief only. Written by the Fable 5 review session (2026-07-01) with the entire book in context — the cross-links below are verified against the real text, not reconstructed from memory. See `notes/fable-review-plan.md` item A for why this chapter is the book's clearest 10x move.

**Author's note to relieve:** Logan has been putting this off because he doesn't feel he deeply understands selfish-predator psychology. **He doesn't need to.** The book already contains all the psychology it needs (blurry predator, "hungry and naive hunters," "selfishness is just unskilled need-seeking"). This is a *protocol* chapter, not a psychology chapter — what to do when the pattern is verified, regardless of what's underneath it. The chapter can and should be honest that you often never learn what was underneath.

## What's Missing From the Book

The book currently has:

- Verification before judgment (`before-you-judge.md` — qualified sources, witch hunts, "When You Can't Verify")
- Proportionality in both directions (`appropriate-response.md` — over- AND under-response)
- Removal rules (`before-you-facilitate.md` — The Promise's five removal conditions, including intentional malice and repeated violations after being told)
- A full crisis walkthrough where the *threat-maker* is a righteous predator (`handling-threats-of-violence.md`)
- The base-rate claim: "Nine times out of ten, when something goes wrong, it's not malice" (`before-you-judge.md`)
- The selfish predator's *indirect* methods — hiding next to the righteous predator, aiming mobs at blind spots (`why-rescuers-are-dangerous.md#the-selfish-predators-best-tool`)
- The three-type triage when reports accumulate: clumsy / harmful belief / malicious — "the least likely of the three, but the one your filters will jump to first" (`appropriate-response.md#report-to-facilitators`)
- "Patterns change severity" — repetition after clear feedback shifts the assessment (`severity.md#patterns-change-severity`)
- "Actual predators get identified and removed quickly" (`before-you-judge.md`) — **asserted but never shown**

What the book does NOT have: **a single worked path from suspicion → pattern → verified malice → proportional HIGH-severity response, executed cleanly, without the community becoming a mob.** Nearly every worked example in 162k words resolves as "it was a mistake." The tenth case — the real one — is asserted, gestured at, and never walked through. This is the book's biggest attack surface ("a manual for making accusers doubt themselves") and one chapter closes it.

## The Core Insight (the opening move)

Go back to the original fable. The tragedy of "The Boy Who Cried Wolf" is not the false alarms. It's that **the false alarms destroyed the village's ability to respond to the real wolf.** The boy cried wolf twice, the village stopped believing, and when the wolf actually came, nobody moved — and the sheep died.

That is this chapter's frame, and it flips the book's entire argument into its final position:

> **Proportionality isn't softness toward wolves. It's what keeps the alarm system credible for the day a wolf actually comes.**

A community that mobs every mistake produces alarm fatigue — [punishment culture](../src/concepts/punishment-culture.md) already shows that everyone learns to hide, report nothing, and distrust accusations. That community is *defenseless* against a real wolf, because a real accusation lands in a room full of people who've watched three innocent people get destroyed and now discount every alarm. The witch hunts don't just hurt the innocent. **They protect the guilty.** A real wolf's best camouflage is a village that cries wolf constantly — and the book already shows wolves exploiting exactly this (`the-selfish-predators-best-tool`).

So the chapter is not a concession bolted on for balance. It's the completion of the argument: everything the book teaches — verify, proportion, protect reporting, refuse mobs — is what makes a community *dangerous to actual predators*. The village that verified a hundred times keeps its sheep, because when the wolf finally comes, everyone believes the alarm and everyone moves.

## What the Chapter Should Cover

### 1. The End of the Fable (opening)

As above. One page. The book has spent thirty chapters on false alarms; this chapter is about the real one — and why the previous thirty chapters are what make this one winnable.

### 2. What a Verified Pattern Looks Like

Not psychology — observable, Notice-level signals, keyed to concepts the reader already has:

- **Repetition after clear feedback** (`severity.md#patterns-change-severity`) — the single strongest signal. One momentary slip is the popcorn metaphor; the same behavior after an explicit boundary, across multiple people, is a pattern.
- **Cross-report convergence** — this is what [protected reporting](../src/concepts/punishment-culture.md#protected-reporting) and [Report to Facilitators](../src/concepts/appropriate-response.md#report-to-facilitators) exist to produce. One report is information; three independent reports of the same behavior with different people is a pattern no single filter invented. (Note the contrast with `before-you-judge.md`'s warning about rumors: the difference is the facilitator verifying each report with primary sources, not hearsay accumulating.)
- **Target selection** — the behavior lands disproportionately on newcomers, fawners, people least likely to report. (Links: `fawning.md`, the safest-place logic inverted — a wolf avoids witnesses; `healing-fawning.md#the-safest-place-in-the-world` already says violating boundaries in front of 30 interveners is the dumbest strategy, so the smart wolf engineers un-witnessed contexts.)
- **Behavior changes under observation** — the mistake-maker's behavior is consistent whether or not staff are watching; a wolf's isn't.
- **The vet-dodge** — refuses or steamrolls the conversations the book teaches (RBDSMT, friction check, hard-boundary confirmation). Someone who won't let you screen is telling you something.
- **The blurry self-statement, heard out loud** — "I'm owed. They have what should be mine. The situation makes it just" (`why-rescuers-are-dangerous.md#the-blurry-predator-where-most-harm-lives`).

Frame explicitly: **you are diagnosing a pattern, not a soul.** You may never know if it's fear, hunger, compulsion, or malice underneath. The protocol doesn't need to know. This is where Logan's real-world example fits — someone whose actions were consistently selfish, where the driver may have been fear (manipulating when afraid). The chapter holds that ambiguity on purpose: *the why matters for compassion; the pattern is what determines the response.* (Pattern only, never identifiable — see `.private-context.md` rules.)

### 3. Verification Without a Witch Hunt

The facilitator as investigator, under maximum pressure to skip steps:

- Primary sources for every report; "I don't know yet" discipline (`before-you-judge.md#when-you-cant-verify`)
- Documentation while it's fresh (parallels `when-youve-been-wronged.md#document-what-actually-happened`)
- **Response severity scales with verification quality** — the existing rule ("if unqualified, any response is over-response") holds even here; the difference is that a verified pattern *qualifies* you for HIGH
- Keeping the investigation from becoming the punishment — no public naming during verification, per the same logic as removing the defamation before the group gathers (`before-you-facilitate.md#what-facilitator-fawning-looks-like`)

### 4. The Proportional HIGH Response, Executed

This is the heart of the chapter — the thing no one has ever written down for these spaces:

- **The removal conversation**: firm, final, and human. Dignity without negotiation. The book's language rules apply *hardest* here — describe the verified behaviors, not monster labels. The chapter must *demonstrate* that you can permanently remove someone without dehumanizing them, which is the book's whole thesis under maximum load. (Contrast anchor: `handling-threats-of-violence.md`'s removal script, which offers a path back. This one may not.)
- **What to tell the group**: facts in Notice language, the pattern (not the diagnosis), the consequence, and the reaffirmation of the Promise — mistakes are still protected; *this was not a mistake, and here is how we know.* The "how we know" is what keeps the room from concluding that anyone could be next. (Anchor: `handling-threats-of-violence.md#what-to-say-to-the-crowd` — same move, harder content.)
- **No re-entry, or a very steep one**: contrast with the righteous predator's re-entry policy (time + genuine repair). A verified pattern of exploiting the container may warrant permanent exclusion — and the chapter should say plainly that this is proportional, not punishment culture. The severity model already supports it: permanence of harm, pattern after feedback.
- **Warning other organizers — the hardest section. DECIDED (2026-07-01): perspective, not prescription.** This is a genuine values call that each facilitator has to make for themselves — reasonable people in this industry disagree about it, and the chapter should say so plainly rather than hand down a universal rule. The chapter's job is the book's signature move: surface the considerations most facilitators have never distinguished, then leave the decision with them. The considerations: mob callouts (unverified stories, spread for punishment) vs. **verified-pattern notification between container-holders** (primary-source-verified, factual, shared for protection, with the same "I don't know / here's what I verified" honesty from `before-you-judge.md#when-you-cant-verify`); the accountability-vs-harm table from `power-dynamics.md#when-does-power-become-harmful` as a function test; reputation damage as HIGH-severity/permanent (`severity.md`) so the verification bar must match; and what silence costs the next container. Still a **prime Field Review Packet question** — ask the facilitator reviewers how their networks actually handle this and what's broken — but as input for sharpening the considerations, not as a vote on a protocol the book will prescribe.
- **When to involve law enforcement**: brief, sober — assault, minors, threats carried out. The book shouldn't pretend community protocol covers crimes.

### 5. The Person Who Was Harmed

- Support without rescue (`drama-triangle.md` Coach; `when-things-go-wrong.md#when-you-see-someone-fawning`)
- Their repair, when the other party is bad-faith: `repair.md#when-repair-isnt-possible` exists but is four lines — this expands it. The pay-it-forward direction inverts: **the community pays it forward to them** (the container covers what the wolf never will: acknowledgment, safety planning, the refund logic from `handling-threats-of-violence.md#the-refund-show-dont-tell` applied here)
- Their filters afterward: one real wolf will try to install "wolves are everywhere." True victims need `the-way-out` (`when-youve-been-wronged.md`) *without* it being used to minimize — the chapter should say explicitly that everything in The Way Out applies AND the wolf was real AND both are true.

### 6. Aftercare: Don't Let the Real Wolf Re-Tune the Village

After a verified incident, every RAS in the community shifts to predator-scanning (`trauma-and-filters.md#when-accusations-occur`) — except this time the confirmation was real, so the filter installs *harder*. The facilitator's job in the following weeks: actively re-frame the base rate. One real wolf doesn't change the math — it was the tenth case, the other nine are still mistakes, and the system *worked*: reporting, verification, proportional response, clean removal. The incident should become evidence that the container can handle the worst — not evidence that everyone was right to be scanning. (Anchors: `before-you-facilitate.md#pre-framing-the-flip-side`, `handling-threats-of-violence.md#aftercare-for-the-container`.)

### 7. Closing: The Village That Can Believe Its Own Alarm

Return to the fable. The test of everything this book teaches is not whether your community is kind to mistake-makers — it's whether, on the day the alarm is real, the village moves as one. Verification a hundred times is what buys that. Possible closing line shape: *the village that cried wolf lost its sheep; the village that verified kept them — because when the wolf finally came, everyone believed the alarm, and everyone moved.*

## Tone

This is the other chapter where the book shows its teeth — the register of `harmless-is-not-peaceful.md`: "precision instruments for the dangerous." Calm, procedural, and absolutely unflinching. No moralizing even here — *especially* here. The chapter's credibility move is demonstrating that HIGH-severity response and human dignity are compatible: you can end someone's access to your community forever, warn your peers, and involve the law — all in Notice language, without once calling them a monster. If the chapter can do that, the whole book becomes inarguable.

## Why This Chapter Protects the Book

Without it, the book's strongest hostile reading is "162,000 words about why accusers are the real problem."
